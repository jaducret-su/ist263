Explanations

Overthinking:

What it is: Overthinking is not a lack of control. It is your brain attempting to prevent future harm by replaying information.

Why it Makes Sense: Your brain is designed to reduce uncertainty. Replaying situations feels productive because repetition signals preparation and safety. It is especially common if:

  • You were rewarded for being prepared
  • Dealt with serious consequences from past mistakes
  • You learned to anticipate others reactions

When it Becomes Costly:

  • Mental Exhaustion
  • Difficulty Sleeping
  • Avoiding Decisions

What Helps:

  • Writing down thoughts to externalize them
  • Learning to tolerate unresolved outcomes
  • Practice making decisions without total certainty

Avoidance:

What it is: Avoidance is not laziness. It is your nervous system trying to protect you from discomfort.

Why it Makes Sense: Avoiding something reduces anxiety in the short term, teaching your brain that avoidance equals safety. It is especially common if:

  • Stress felt overwhelming
  • Failure carried emotional consequences
  • You learned relief comes from disengaging

When it Becomes Costly:

  • Shrinking opportunities
  • Increased anxiety over time
  • Loss of confidence
  • Feeling stuck or behind

What Helps:

  • Gradual exposure rather than forcing action
  • Reducing pressure to "feel ready"
  • Focusing on progress, not comfort
  • Supportive accountability

People Pleasing:

What it is: People-pleasing is a survival strategy, not a personality flaw.

Why it Makes Sense: Maintaining harmony can feel necessary when approval, safety, or stability depended on others’ reactions. This is especially common if:

  • Conflict felt unsafe
  • Approval was conditional
  • You learned to prioritize others' needs over your own

When it Becomes Costly:

  • Resentment
  • Loss of identity
  • Burnout
  • Difficulty setting boundaries

What Helps:

  • Practicing small acts of assertiveness
  • Allowing discomfort without fixing it
  • Reconnecting with personal values
  • Learning that disagreement can be healthy

Emotional Numbness:

What it is: Emotional numbness is a protective shutdown, not a lack of emotion.

Why it Makes Sense: When emotions feel overwhelming or unsafe, the nervous system may dampen them to maintain functioning. This is especially common if:

  • Emotional expression was not supported
  • Stress was chronic
  • Vulnerability felt risky

When it Becomes Costly:

  • Difficulty experiencing joy
  • LDisconnection from others
  • Feeling empty or detached
  • Trouble identifying needs and emotions

What Helps:

  • Gentle emotional awareness
  • Reducing self-pressure to feel in control
  • Physical regulation such as movement and breathing
  • Safe relational connection
